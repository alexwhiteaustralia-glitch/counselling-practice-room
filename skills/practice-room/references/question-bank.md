# Question bank

A bank of lines organised by session phase. Each line is tagged with the card it belongs to. The bank assumes a 50-minute session; in short practice sessions, compress the opening and ending to a line each.

## Opening: making contact (Opening card)

- "How are you arriving today?"
- "I wonder what's been sitting with you this week." ("I wonder what" opens gently; see Reflection stems)
- "Let's take a moment to land before we get into it." (also Presence)
- "Where would you like to start?"
- "What's been with you since we last met?" (ongoing work, not a first session)
- "We've got about 50 minutes. I'll keep an eye on time so you don't have to."
- "If anything I say doesn't fit, tell me. You're the expert on your experience."

## Goal setting (Opening and Focusing cards)

- "How would you know if this has been a useful session?"
- "If you walked out feeling this was worth your time, what would have shifted?"
- "Of everything you've mentioned, what feels most important to look at today?" (focusing)
- "What made now the time to come and talk about this?"
- "What would you like from me in this?"
- "You've named a few things. Which one is asking for attention most?" (focusing)

## Exploring: listening and reflecting (Levels 1 and 2)

- "Tell me more about that."
- "What was that like for you?"
- "What's it like right now, as you tell me?" (phenomenological)
- "Can you give me an example of when that happens?"
- "Let me check I'm following you..." then summarise
- "It sounds like part of you wants to stay and part of you is already halfway out the door." (reflecting ambivalence)
- "I noticed you smiled as you said that." (immediacy)
- "What do you make of what's been happening?" (culture and worldview)
- "Who else has a view on this, in your family or community?" (culture and worldview)

## Deepening the process (Levels 2 to 4; Silence card)

- "What's happening in your body as you say that?" (see the Reflecting feeling scope note; check the timing)
- "Stay with that for a moment." (Presence)
- "What's underneath the frustration?" (only once frustration has been named by the client)
- "Something you haven't let yourself feel finding a way out." (advanced empathy, for darker material)
- "It's like something underneath slipped past you for a second." (advanced empathy, gentler)
- "That part of you that wants to hold back, what do you think it's trying to keep safe?" (Polarities)
- "If that feeling had a voice, what would it say?" (Self-criticism)
- "There's that voice again, right now." (Self-criticism and Immediacy; when the critic shows up live in the room)
- "When have you felt this before?" (Re-experiencing; only after the present feeling has been met)
- "What does it mean to you that this keeps happening?" (Reflection of meaning)
- "On one hand you've said..., and on the other..." (Confronting)
- Silence. Often the strongest move in this phase.

## Ambivalence and change (Working with ambivalence card)

Use these once both sides of the ambivalence have been heard and reflected. They draw out change talk; they don't replace reflecting it.

Exploring both sides:

- "What's good about things the way they are?" (benefits of not changing; ask this side first, it lowers defences)
- "What concerns you about how things are going?"
- "What would be different if you did make a change?"

Rulers:

- "On a scale of 0 to 10, how important is this to you right now?" (importance)
- "What makes it a [number] and not a [lower number]?" (the follow-up that draws out change talk; never "why not higher?")
- "How confident are you, 0 to 10, that you could do it if you decided to?" (confidence)
- "What would it take to move from a [number] to a [number plus one]?"

Looking forward, looking back, values:

- "If nothing changes, where do you see this in a few years?"
- "What was life like before this became a pattern?"
- "What matters most to you? Where does this fit with that?"

Giving information (Ask-Offer-Ask):

- "What do you already know about...?"
- "Would it be okay if I shared something I've seen?"
- "What do you make of that?"

Responding to discord and sustain talk:

- "So there's nothing about it that bothers you at all." (amplified reflection; plain, never sarcastic)
- "It's your call. Nobody else can decide this for you." (emphasising autonomy)
- "Sounds like you've had a lot of people on your back about this." (reframing the pressure)
- "We don't have to sort that today. What feels most pressing?" (shifting focus)

Moving toward planning (only once change talk is strong):

- "Where does this leave you?" (open enough to allow "nowhere yet")
- "What, if anything, would be a first step?"

## Ending the session (Ending card)

Signalling and gathering:

- "We've got about ten minutes left. Let's start bringing this together." (two minutes in a short practice session)
- "Can I pull together what we've talked about?"

Closes that don't ask anything (prefer these):

- Hand back their own phrase, then stop: "Stop waiting." "Feeling your way into it."
- Name the shift: "Something settled just then."
- Mark the arc: "You came in not sure you wanted to be here, and you're leaving with something to work with."
- Affirm the effort: "It took something to say all that out loud."
- Simple close: "We'll pick this up next week."

Closing questions (use one, and not straight after a reflection that's still landing):

- "What are you taking away from today?"
- "How are you leaving compared to how you arrived?"
- "We went to some big places today. What will help you look after yourself this week?"
- "How was today for you? Anything you'd want me to do differently next time?" (alliance check, worth using every session)

## Using the bank well

- A line from the bank is a starting point. Adapt it to the client's own words.
- Exploring and deepening lines land best straight after a reflection, not instead of one.
- One question at a time, then leave space.
- Watch for a pet line. If the same question turns up after most reflections ("How does that sit?"), it has become a reflex rather than a choice.
